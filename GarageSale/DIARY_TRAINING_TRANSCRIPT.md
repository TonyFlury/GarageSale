# Garage Sale Diary training transcript

Diary package version: `0.1.5.1`.

Voice: natural synthetic British English narration, `en-GB-SoniaNeural`.

Orange outlines identify controls used in the recorded application.

## 00:00:00 — Your tasks, in one place

Welcome to the Garage Sale Diary. This walkthrough uses version zero point one point five point one, and the Garage Sale calendar example. We start as Alex, a Diary User. The demonstration contains late tasks, upcoming work, and blocked activities. Orange highlights identify the controls we use.

## 00:00:24 — Late does not always mean blocked

My Diary shows tasks assigned to you and your current groups. A task can be ready and late, or blocked and late. In this version, late means incomplete and due today or earlier. Ready means its predecessors are finished. Upcoming ready tasks remain available before their due date.

## 00:00:47 — Filter the list

The default list hides blocked tasks. Select show blocked also, then Apply filters, to include them. Include whole-team tasks brings shared team work into your list. These choices change what you see. They do not change a task or complete any work.

## 00:01:08 — Use a specific status

Use Task status for All open tasks, Late only, Ready and not late, or Blocked and not late. The status links provide quick alternatives. Late takes priority in the counts, so a late blocked task belongs in Late. Clear filters restores the default view.

## 00:01:29 — Add your own task

Choose Add task to create a personal reminder. Ordinary users create tasks for themselves. They cannot assign another owner or add their task to the shared template. Personal additions are private from other ordinary users, but Diary Managers can inspect them. A private task cannot be used as a dependency.

## 00:01:53 — Choose the name and date rule

Enter a clear task name and a short description. Here we create a reminder to call the printing company. Choose One-time date and enter an exact date. Milestone and annual rules are also available. Complete only the fields for the selected rule. There is no dependency editor on this form.

## 00:02:16 — Inspect your saved task

Return to My Diary and open the new task. Its source is Diary addition. It belongs to Alex, has no open predecessors, and is ready. Personal tasks do not change the template or other event diaries. This is a reminder for this diary only.

## 00:02:37 — Change a personal due date

You can change the due date of your own open personal addition. Set the revised date, then choose Save due date. This affects this diary only. Ordinary users cannot change dates on copied template tasks. A completed task cannot have its due date changed.

## 00:02:58 — Keep a useful note

Use Notes to record progress or information for the next action. Enter the note, then select Add note. The diary records its author and time. Notes are added to the history rather than replacing previous notes. Assigned users and managers can add notes, including after completion.

## 00:03:21 — Complete finished work

When the work is finished, select Complete. The task records who completed it, when it was completed, and its due date at completion. Completion removes it from the personal open-task list. A note alone does not complete a task. You can still add a follow-up note to the completed record.

## 00:03:44 — Understand an open predecessor

Now open Open advertising bookings. It is blocked by Prepare advertising bookings. Dependencies specify the work that must finish first. Check Open predecessors before trying to complete a blocked activity. In this example, the predecessor is also late. A task can therefore be blocked even when its own date is in the future.

## 00:04:10 — Blocked work cannot complete normally

If we select Complete while the predecessor remains open, the diary rejects the action. Nothing is completed. Read the message, then resolve the earlier work. Diary Users cannot force the chain to complete. The manager Close all action will be demonstrated later.

## 00:04:32 — Record progress on shared work

Open Prepare advertising bookings, the earlier task in the chain. Alex belongs to its assigned group and can add a note. Record what was checked before completion. The task is late, but it is ready because it has no unfinished predecessors. Its template date is not editable by this ordinary user.

## 00:04:55 — Complete the predecessor

Choose Complete on the preparation task. This records completion of that task only. The diary recalculates the dependent activity from the saved dependency chain. Completing the preparation does not automatically complete Open advertising bookings. It makes that next task available to finish normally.

## 00:05:19 — The next task is now ready

Open advertising bookings is now ready and has no open predecessors. Review its details and perform the work before selecting Complete. The due date has not moved. A newly unblocked task can still be late. This is how normal task progression works through the example template.

## 00:05:41 — Review the full event diary

We now switch to Morgan, a Diary Manager. The sidebar includes Manager Diary and Templates. Manager Diary shows the full permitted event diary, including personal additions. Managers can inspect task details, record notes, create tasks with owners, change permitted dates, and recover orphaned template assignments.

## 00:06:07 — Manager filters

The manager has three filters: include completed tasks, exclude blocked tasks, and exclude non-late tasks. Apply filters combines the selected choices. Here we include history and hide blocked work. Completed tasks remain history and are never marked late or blocked. Clear filters restores the standard open-task list.

## 00:06:33 — Find late tasks

Select exclude non-late tasks and apply the filter to focus on overdue work. A late task is incomplete and due today or earlier. Filters do not resolve the work or remove dependencies. Clear filters before reviewing the full diary again.

## 00:06:52 — Orphaned template tasks appear first

Close advertising bookings is at the top because its original owner is no longer eligible. For training, Jamie was assigned this template task and then made inactive. Only template tasks qualify as orphaned. A manager can leave one unresolved, but it remains unfinished and its dependent tasks remain blocked.

## 00:07:16 — Choose a replacement owner

Open the orphaned task to use its normal editor. Choose a new owner type. Person offers eligible active Diary Users. Ownership group offers authorized groups. Whole team makes it shared team work. Here we select Alex as the replacement. This recovery control is for an open orphaned template task, not a general reassignment tool.

## 00:07:44 — Recovery preserves the task

The task keeps its identity, due date, and dependency chain. It is no longer orphaned. Reassigning this diary task does not rewrite the source template or other existing diaries. Review template ownership separately when planning future events. Add a note explaining the handover.

## 00:08:06 — Change a template task due date

Managers can change the due date of an open copied template task. Here we move the final advertising check. Save due date changes this diary only; the original milestone rule remains visible. This version does not warn if a date override creates a scheduling conflict. Check dependencies and dates yourself.

## 00:08:31 — Create a manager-assigned task

From Manager Diary, choose Add task. Managers can assign a person, an authorized ownership group, or Whole team. We create a shared equipment check for the Executive Member group. The task remains a diary addition unless the separate template option is explicitly selected.

## 00:08:52 — Keep diary work separate from templates

The lower section offers Add to template for future diaries. That is a separate decision and requires a reusable date rule. We leave it off and save this task only. Existing diary dependencies remain fixed, and the new task is not inserted into an existing dependency chain.

## 00:09:14 — Inspect the blocking chain first

Open Complete final event checks. It is still blocked by advertising and sale-booking work. The Open predecessors section lists the unfinished chain. A manager should inspect it before using Close all. Normal Complete still requires predecessors to finish. Close all is the privileged alternative.

## 00:09:38 — Close all completes the open chain

Close all completes the selected task and its open predecessor chain together. It does not merely hide a warning or remove dependencies. Use it only when that completion is intended. We choose it now for this demonstration. The diary records Morgan as the acting manager.

## 00:10:00 — Review the completion history

Return to Manager Diary and include completed tasks. The completed records show who acted and when. Open a predecessor to see the Close all history comment, which explains why it was force-completed. The final task Run the Garage Sale remains separate: completing its predecessor makes it ready, not completed.

## 00:10:24 — Audit a forced predecessor

This predecessor records a forced completion for Complete final event checks. The earlier note is retained. Completion history and notes provide context for the next manager. Read the record before taking another action, and reload a task if the diary reports that its saved state has changed.

## 00:10:47 — A practical daily routine

For everyday work, check Late, inspect blockers, add useful notes, and complete work in order. Use personal tasks for your own reminders. Managers review orphaned tasks, apply filters, and use date or ownership recovery carefully. Close all is an explicit completion of the chain. This concludes the Garage Sale Diary walkthrough.
